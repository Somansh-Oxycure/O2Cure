The "last modified" dates need reading carefully. Thirty-two of the thirty-five posts show a modification date in 2022, clustered in February, April and June. That pattern — dozens of posts touched within days of each other — is the signature of a bulk technical operation such as a migration or a plugin-driven metadata sweep, not of anyone revisiting the content. The only posts never touched after publication are the three most recent ones. In practice, no post in this archive has had a genuine editorial refresh.

Thirteen of the thirty-five posts name COVID, coronavirus, SARS-CoV-2 or a pandemic wave in the title itself. Twenty-seven of the thirty-five mention it somewhere in the body. Only eight posts are entirely free of it.

| MEASURE | COUNT | POSTS |

This matters in two different ways. A handful of posts are simply dated news — whether the third wave had started, what the prevention guidelines were — and those are dead weight that should be removed. But a larger group is more frustrating: posts about genuinely evergreen subjects that happen to be wrapped in COVID framing. A post explaining what a HEPA filter removes is useful forever; a post titled "Do Air Purifiers With HEPA Filter Capture The Coronavirus?" dates itself in the headline. Those are rewrites, not retirements, and they are where most of the recoverable value sits.

## **1.4  Keyword overlap and cannibalisation**

When several posts target the same query, they split their own authority and the search engine picks one more or less arbitrarily. The archive has nine identifiable clusters, four of which contain outright duplicates.

**Commercial buying guides  —  posts 4, 11, 12, 14, 15, 32**

Six posts chase overlapping "best / which air purifier should I buy" intent. Posts 4 and 11 both carry "2022" in the title and both target the same head term. Post 32 is a thinner restatement of post 11\.

* **Recommendation:** Rewrite 11 as the single evergreen buying guide, rewrite 4 as the Delhi-specific variant, merge 32 into 11, keep 12 for odour and 14 for the do-I-need-one question, rebuild 15 as the competitor comparison.

**PHI / REME technology  —  posts 33, 34, 35**

Three posts published within ten days of each other explaining the same RGF PHI-Cell and REME HALO technology, each citing the same inactivation claims.

* **Recommendation:** Consolidate into post 35 as the canonical technology page. 301 both others.

**HEPA explainers  —  posts 8, 9**

Two HEPA posts written three days apart, one framed around coronavirus capture and one around general health benefits.

* **Recommendation:** Rewrite 8 de-COVIDed as the canonical HEPA page and merge 9 into it.

**Dental  —  posts 7, 24**

Two posts on air purification for dental practices. Post 7 is 1,266 words with 13 subheads; post 24 is 712 words framed as a COVID protective measure.

* **Recommendation:** Keep 7, merge 24 into it.

**Humidifiers  —  posts 2, 13, 19**

Post 2 is a strong standalone humidifier explainer; 13 is a thin COVID-framed piece; 19 covers running a purifier and humidifier together.

* **Recommendation:** Keep 2 and 19 as distinct pages, merge 13 into 2\.

**COVID-era news and guidance  —  posts 17, 18, 20, 22, 23, 29**

Six posts pinned to specific 2021 moments — the third wave, prevention guidelines, aerosol transmission, relaxed pollution limits. None has evergreen search value and several now contain guidance that has been superseded.

* **Recommendation:** Retire all six with 301s to the nearest live post.

**B2B verticals  —  posts 7, 16, 26, 27, 28, 30**

The vertical set (dental, buildings, gyms, hotels, restaurants, schools) is the closest thing the blog has to B2B content, but every post was written in 2021 as a COVID reopening play and none exceeds 1,200 words.

* **Recommendation:** This is the largest opportunity in the archive. Rebuild as a proper B2B vertical hub, each page written for a facilities or procurement reader rather than a homeowner.

**Health conditions and pollutants  —  posts 5, 6, 21, 25**

Allergies, cigarette smoke, PM2.5 and cardiovascular risk, sinus relief. Little overlap between them — this is the healthiest cluster in the archive.

* **Recommendation:** Refresh all four, and add product links, which none of them has enough of.

**Air-quality education  —  posts 10, 16, 31**

Cost of pollution, fresh air in modern buildings, seasonality of pollution. All top-of-funnel and all still relevant.

* **Recommendation:** Refresh; make 31 the seasonal anchor and 16 the B2B anchor.

## **1.8  The strategic gap**

Set aside the age of the content for a moment. Judged against where the business is heading, the archive is pointed in the wrong direction.

* **B2C-heavy.** Almost every post is written for a homeowner choosing a room purifier. The six vertical posts (dental, gyms, hotels, restaurants, schools, modern buildings) are the only B2B content, none exceeds 1,200 words, and all six were written in 2021 as COVID reopening plays rather than as procurement-grade material.

* **No commercial-grade content.** There is no content at all on the commercial and industrial range — no case studies, no sizing or CADR guidance for large spaces, no installation or maintenance material, nothing addressed to a facilities manager or a consultant.

* **No comparison content.** Only one post (No. 15, and it is 164 words) addresses a competitor by name. For a category where buyers compare Dyson, Philips, Mi, Kent and Honeywell before purchasing, that is a large uncovered set of high-intent queries.

* **Moscure absent.** Nothing in the archive covers the mosquito-control category at all, so Moscure has no content footprint whatsoever.

* **Seasonality under-built.** Post 31, on the seasonality of air pollution, is the most commercially aligned idea in the library — and it is 537 words with one image. The October-to-January demand peak deserves a cluster, not a footnote.

## **1.10  Recommended sequence**

Ordered by return on effort rather than by post number. The first three items are a few days of work and address most of the damage.

**Week 1 — stop the bleeding**

1. Retitle posts 4 and 11 to remove "2022", and update the product line-up and prices inside them. Two posts, immediate click-through gain, no new writing.

2. Re-host the nine googleusercontent images on posts 15 and 16 onto o2cure.in before they break.

**Week 2 — consolidate**

1. Retire the six dead COVID posts (17, 18, 20, 22, 23, 29\) with 301 redirects. Removing them raises the average quality of everything that remains.

2. Execute the six merges: 9 into 8, 13 into 2, 24 into 7, 32 into 11, and 33 and 34 into 35\. Redirect each merged URL.

3. Fix the three over-length SEO titles and the twelve over-length meta descriptions.

**Weeks 3 to 6 — rebuild the commercial core**

1. Rewrite post 11 as the single evergreen "how to choose an air purifier in India" guide, absorbing post 32\.

2. Rewrite post 8 as the canonical HEPA explainer, absorbing post 9, with the COVID framing removed.

3. Rebuild post 15 as a real competitor comparison, and add the Dyson, Philips, Mi and Honeywell comparisons that do not exist yet.

4. Expand post 31 into the seasonal anchor, and plan the cluster around it in time for the October pollution peak.

**Ongoing — the B2B gap**

1. Rewrite the four vertical posts that need it (27 hotels, 28 restaurants, 30 schools, plus 26 gyms expanded) for a facilities and procurement reader rather than a homeowner.

2. Make post 16 the anchor of a B2B set, and add the commercial-range content that does not exist at all: sizing and CADR guidance for large spaces, installation and maintenance, and case studies.

3. Open a Moscure content track. The category currently has zero footprint.

4. Re-establish a publishing cadence. Two posts a month sustained beats twenty-three in a year followed by four years of near-silence.

**POST CONTENT, VERBATIM**

When it comes to respiratory illnesses, most of us are familiar with the common cold or flu. However, there's a lesser-known yet impactful virus making its presence felt: Human Metapneumovirus (HMPV).
